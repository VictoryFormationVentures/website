A first look

In Westlake, on Lake Austin and in the gated Hill Country communities, many of the best homes change hands quietly. Members of The Limestone Collection see our private exclusives first, and because we work with builders and landowners every week, we hear about homes before they are listed at all.

02

New construction is our home turf

Texas does not license home builders. There is no state board checking who builds your house, so the vetting is on you, or on us. We check a builder's record, finished homes, warranty and insurance before you sign anything.

03

A real number before you offer

The price is not the cost. We put a number on the year one tax bill, the MUD, the HOA, insurance, the allowances and the upkeep that comes with acreage, pools and water, before you are under contract.

What it costs to hold

Texas taxes the house, not the paycheck

With no income tax, property tax does the work, and the rate depends on the exact address: county, city, school district and any special districts stacked together. The luxury west side carries some of the lowest combined rates in the metro.

Your first bill is on full market value. The 10% annual cap on a homestead's taxable value only starts the year after you qualify, so we estimate year one on your purchase price, not the seller's old bill.

The water

Two lakes, two different promises

Lake Austin is a constant level lake. The water at your dock in August is the water you saw at the showing.

Lake Travis is a flood control reservoir, and its level can swing by tens of feet between drought and flood. A dock that floats in a wet spring can sit on dry ground in a dry year. We pull the lake level history for the address and check the dock permits with the LCRA or the City of Austin before you fall in love with the view.

Areas and developments
New construction

The builder's contract is not the standard one

Resales use the Texas Real Estate Commission's promulgated forms, written to be fair to both sides. Many builders use their own contract instead, and it is written for them.

We read it with you line by line: who holds your deposit, what the allowances really buy, the markup on change orders, the completion date and what happens if it slips, the warranty, and the arbitration clause. Allowances are where a $2 million contract quietly becomes $2.3 million.

If something goes wrong later, Texas law requires written notice to the builder at least 60 days before you can sue, and gives them the right to inspect and offer a repair first. We make sure the paper trail starts at the walkthrough.

In fairness

The honest caveat

Texas is a non-disclosure state. Sale prices are not public record, so you cannot look up what the house next door sold for, and private exclusives have no public price history at all.

That makes it harder to know whether an asking price is fair. Our job is to close that gap with the actual closing record from the MLS, and to tell you plainly when a house is overpriced, wrong for you, or carrying a problem the photos do not show. We would rather lose a deal than put you in the wrong home.

Know what it costs in year two, not just on closing day.

Representation, money and closing

Straight answers

What representation costs

Since January 1, 2026, Texas law requires a written buyer agreement before an agent shows you a home. It states what your agent is paid, how long the agreement runs and how you can end it. We put all of it in writing before the first tour, and the fee is negotiable.

Who pays it

Often the seller or builder offers to cover it, outside the MLS. If they offer less than our agreed fee, the difference can be asked for in your offer or paid at closing. We decide the structure with you before you write an offer.

The option period

Most Texas contracts give you a paid option period, a few days to inspect and walk away for any reason. It is your most important protection on a resale, and we plan the inspections, sewer scope, survey and insurance quotes to fit inside it.

Financing and proof of funds

Above $832,750 the loan is jumbo, and underwriting takes longer. Sellers at this level expect a strong pre-approval or proof of funds with the offer. We introduce lenders who do jumbo and construction loans and tell you what can be redacted.

Who handles the closing

Texas closings run through a title company, not an attorney. Title premiums are set by the state, so the price is the same everywhere. For a builder's contract or acreage with wells, easements or ag exemptions, we still recommend your own attorney.

Wells, septic and land

West of the city many homes run on a private well and septic system, and some land carries an agricultural valuation that can be lost, with back taxes, when use changes. We check all three before your option period ends.
